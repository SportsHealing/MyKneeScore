Track progress

One number, watched over time.

A single score is a snapshot. A series of scores is a story. Every time you take a test, your new score joins the ones before it, so you can see whether your knee is getting better or worse.

How it works

Four steps, no account needed

  1. Take a questionnaire

    Answer the questions on the main test or any of the Advanced scores. You get your score straight away.

  2. Keep your results

    At the end, download your results as a PDF, email them to yourself, or both. Add your name to the PDF if you like.

  3. Find your personal link

    Towards the bottom of your PDF or email there is a link made just for you. It carries your scores so far.

  4. Use that link next time

    Retake the test from your link. Your new score is added to the "Track your progress" table at the bottom of your new PDF or email, with the change since last time.

  • Always use your latest link. Each new PDF or email holds all your scores so far, up to your last 12.
  • One link per test. Your Short Knee Score link tracks the Short Knee Score. Each Advanced score has its own link.
  • Your scores stay with you. Nothing is saved on this website. Your history lives inside your own link, so keep your latest PDF or email somewhere safe.

Reading the trend

Progress or deterioration, at a glance

  • Retest every two to four weeks during treatment or recovery, and whenever something changes.
  • A rise of 10 points or more is a meaningful improvement.
  • A steady fall is a signal to review your plan with a clinician.

Your score

What the number means

The Short Knee Score uses four bands, each with a clear next step. The bands are a guide, not a diagnosis. Sudden injury, locking, giving way or a hot swollen knee always deserve prompt medical review, whatever the score. Each Advanced score explains its own range on its page.

85–100
Optimal

Your knee is working well. Keep active, keep your legs strong, and retest if anything changes.

70–84
Good

Your knee works well most of the time. A structured six week strengthening programme usually closes the gap. Retest monthly.

60–69
Fair

Symptoms are limiting you. See a physiotherapist or GP. Options include supervised rehabilitation, weight support and, for some, an injection.

0–59
Pay attention

This is affecting your daily life quite significantly. You may require a specialist review and possibly some imaging such as an MRI scan.
