Why it matters

Women's knees are not small men's knees

Female athletes and patients face some knee problems more often, and recover in a body that standard knee scores do not ask about.

Higher injury risk

Studies report that female athletes tear their anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) two to eight times more often than male athletes in the same sports. Kneecap pain is also more common in women.

Hormones can play a part

Some women notice their knee pain, swelling or stiffness changes across the menstrual cycle, with contraception, in pregnancy or around the menopause. FK21 records these so they can be discussed.

Energy, iron and bone

Low energy, iron deficiency and low bone density can slow rehabilitation and training. They are easy to miss unless someone asks.

Pelvic floor and whole-body symptoms

Leaking when running, jumping or landing, night sweats and aches in other joints can all limit rehab and sport. FK21 asks about each one.

Female ACL injuries: what the research shows

More injuries, and a slower return to sport

Our systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 25 studies of 7,183 athletes (2,892 female, 4,291 male) after ACL reconstruction.

2 to 8×more likely to tear the ACL than male athletes
28%lower odds of returning to sport within two years of ACL reconstruction
55 to 65%of all athletes return to their pre-injury level of sport
  • The gap is in the first two years. Female athletes were less likely to have returned to sport at 12 months and up to 24 months. In the longer term, return rates were similar for women and men.
  • Re-tear rates and time to return were similar. So the gap is likely driven by factors that can be changed, such as rehabilitation, confidence and support, alongside biological differences.
  • That makes the first two years the time to watch. Female-specific rehabilitation and psychological support matter most in this window.

Source: Elliott DA, Gill SS, Gupte CM. Are there sex disparities in return to sport after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction? A systematic review and exploratory meta-analysis. American Journal of Sports Medicine. 2026;54(12):3070-3080. doi:10.1177/03635465261448489. The injury rate and the overall return to sport figure come from published studies cited in the review.

Why women are at higher risk

Six factors behind the gap

Anatomy

Women tend to have a narrower notch in the thigh bone where the ACL sits, and an ACL with a 20 to 30% smaller cross-section. A wider pelvis angles the knee inwards. Genetic factors are thought to contribute to around 69% of ACL ruptures.

Movement and muscle control

Women more often land with the knee collapsing inwards and less trunk control. They tend to rely on the quadriceps more than the hamstrings, which protect the ACL. The hamstrings can take 50 to 180 milliseconds to react; the ACL can fail in 40 to 105.

Confidence and fear

Female athletes report more fear of re-injury and score lower on psychological readiness to return. This is one of the key reasons for lower return to sport. You can measure it with the ACL-RSI.

Hormones

Oestrogen increases ligament laxity. ACL tear risk appears highest in the late follicular and ovulatory phases of the cycle. Some studies suggest combined oral contraceptives, which smooth these peaks, may lower ACL injury risk.

Pitches and boots

Female athletes often train on lower quality and artificial pitches, where ACL injuries are reported to be about 50% more likely than on natural grass. Most football boots are designed around male feet and loading.

Surgery choices

In women, hamstring grafts have been linked with a higher failure rate than patellar tendon grafts, and grafts wider than 8 mm do better. Adding a lateral extra-articular tenodesis lowers re-tear risk in elite athletes.

What it asks

21 questions, about six to eight minutes

Your knee (10 questions)

Pain, swelling, sleep, stability, stairs, landing, changing direction, activity level, confidence and overall function. These give your knee score out of 100.

About you (5 questions)

Hormonal status, your cycle, contraception, HRT and related conditions. Recorded with your result, not scored.

Female health (6 questions)

Fatigue, pelvic floor symptoms, night sweats, other joints and changes across your cycle. These give a separate female health score out of 100.

Who should complete it

Female athletes and patients with a knee problem

  • Athletes and active women and girls, especially in pivoting and jumping sports, during rehab or before returning to sport.
  • Patients before and after knee treatment or surgery, to track recovery alongside female health factors.
  • Women in perimenopause or after the menopause with knee pain, where joint aches and poor sleep often overlap.
  • Your results stay private. Nothing you enter is saved on this website. Download a PDF or email it to yourself, and use your personal link to track your scores over time. How tracking works.
  • New and awaiting validation. Treat your result as a guide, and share it with your doctor or physiotherapist.
