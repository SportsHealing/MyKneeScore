Paediatrics

Knee scores for children and teenagers.

Young knees are still growing. They get different injuries, heal differently and need questionnaires written for their age. These scores are designed and tested in children and teenagers.

Start the Pedi-IKDCSee all paediatric scores

How young knees differ

Not just a smaller adult knee

Children and teenagers have open growth plates and growing bones, tendons and ligaments. That changes which injuries happen, how they are treated and how recovery is measured.

Growth plates

The growth plates at the lower end of the thigh bone and the top of the shin bone make much of the growth of the leg. Injuries near them need care, because damage can affect how the leg grows.

Different injuries

Growth spurts bring conditions such as Osgood-Schlatter disease below the kneecap. Teenagers also see kneecap dislocations, osteochondritis dissecans and, more and more, ACL tears. Younger children may break off the bony attachment of the ACL instead of tearing it.

Different treatment

Surgery, such as ACL reconstruction, may be planned to protect the growth plates. Rehabilitation follows growth, school and sport, and return to sport is judged carefully.

Different questions

Adult knee scores ask about work and adult activities, and use harder words. Children's scores ask about school, play and sport in plain language, and are tested in children, so the results can be trusted.

Pedi-IKDC

Pediatric IKDC Subjective Knee Evaluation Form

The most widely used and best studied knee score for children. Covers symptoms, sport and daily function. Ages 10 to 18.

14 questions · 5 to 8 min · 0 to 100, higher is better Take the Pedi-IKDC

Pedi-SKV

Pediatric Simple Knee Value

One question: how your knee compares with a normal knee. A quick check between longer questionnaires.

1 question · under 1 min · 0 to 100, higher is better Take the Pedi-SKV
